## Verdict (plain English, five sentences max, for a non-technical founder)

About half of the Day-1 list is truly closed: strangers can no longer list other people's takes, upload files, read the database with the public key, or award themselves their own job with one account, and those are real wins. The other half is partly done or done in a way that will not last, and one claim is wrong in a way that matters today: the deadline cap is 3 days, not 7, so the job form's own default (7 days) is rejected by the server, and every job posted since Sept 19 has also triggered a duplicate "3 days left" email blast within seconds of the "new job" alert. The money engine underneath was not touched: a cancel can still record a captured charge as refunded with no refund, two clicks can still disagree about who got paid, and card captures and transfers now run inside the public jobs page, on whatever traffic happens to arrive. The new "hide the master until award" feature does not hide the work: producers get a full-length 192 kbps MP3 of every take by design, can derive the WAV from the preview link, and on Vercel the preview step almost certainly never runs, which hands them the master outright. Not safe for live money or for musicians' files yet; the fix order at the end is short at the top and long at the bottom on purpose.

## Grade

### Fixed?
Of the eleven Day-1 items plus the four extras the developer listed, seven are closed as asked (takes listing auth, buyer-not-seller guard, admin needs a confirmed email, RLS migration, hello@ reply-to, the pool URL, Google SSO), six are partly closed (upload signer, the `next` redirect, storage URL prefix, seed guards, webhook hardening, error sanitising), and one is claimed wrong (the deadline cap). "Partly" is doing real work in that sentence: the upload signer still accepts any bytes labelled `application/octet-stream` and now forces the bucket public on every upload; the redirect helper exists but the OAuth callback does not use it and the helper itself falls to a tab character; the webhook change he called "payment status transitions hardened" is the one half of that item that was not done. Three of the seven closed items (RLS, pool, SSO) depend on dashboard settings nobody has shown evidence of, so treat them as "closed in code" until the runtime checks below are run. Net: roughly 50% truly closed, 40% partial, 10% wrong, and every Sept-4 HIGH in the money engine (E-8, E-9, E-14, E-15) is still open.

### Efficient?
The instincts were often right: the self-award guard lives in `lib/jobActions.ts` so both award paths and the sweep inherit it; the redirect, storage-prefix and seed-guard checks are shared helpers; RLS is a migration, which is the right layer. The execution then leaks: the redirect helper has four callers and three use it; the admin confirmed-email predicate is written twice (`lib/admin.ts:41-42` and `app/api/auth/me/route.ts:22`); the deadline cap is three constants in three files with different values and a dead branch; bucket configuration (`updateBucket({ public: true })`) was moved into a request path instead of out of one; `fluent-ffmpeg` is a dependency nothing imports; an 850 KB `tsconfig.tsbuildinfo` is committed; two routes load shared helpers with `await import` for no stated reason. Nothing here is expensive to fix, but none of it was tidied before being called done.

### Logical and future-proof?
No. A new `/api/*` route inherits no protection (middleware gates four page prefixes only, `middleware.ts:32-36`), so the next route added is exactly as exposed as the two that produced E-2 and E-4. The next Prisma table will be readable with the anon key because the RLS migration sets no default privileges. The next upload path will pass the prefix check because the check is bucket-wide, not owner-scoped. The next person who makes the bucket private in the Supabase dashboard will be undone by the next upload. The money sweep runs inside a public GET, so the next feature added to the sweep also runs for anonymous visitors. There are zero tests, so none of the Day-1 guards has a regression detector; the 3-day-versus-7-day mistake is the kind of thing a single test would have caught before it reached production.

### Overall: C+ - real, good-faith progress on the cheap items; the expensive items untouched; one claim wrong in a way that is breaking job posting today; nothing has a test.

## Opinion: Checkout capture-on-post

**Does it fix the root problem?** Yes. The root problem is a timer: a manual-capture authorization dies at 7 days (sooner for some issuers and methods), the product wants jobs open for up to 7 days plus a grace, and every mitigation since Sept 4 (3-day cap, 24-hour finalize grace, 6-day hold guard, lazy auto-finalize) is scaffolding around that timer. Charging at post removes the timer entirely: jobs can stay open as long as the founders like, nothing needs to "finalize before the hold dies", and the sweep stops being a money-critical path. It also lines up with how every comparable marketplace (Upwork, Fiverr, SoundBetter) works, and it protects the party the founders are: the musician's payment is already in the platform's balance when they start recording. Stripe Checkout specifically buys Apple Pay, Link and stronger Radar signals with little code; the same outcome is reachable with the Payment Element the site already has by dropping `capture_method: "manual"`, and I would not treat the redirect-to-Checkout part as essential.

**What it trades away.** The creator's money is actually charged while musicians work, so "cancel for a full refund" becomes a real refund of settled money: Stripe keeps its processing fee on refunds, so under the current product promise every cancellation costs the platform about 3% plus 30 cents, and the free-audition problem in D-11 turns from a privacy issue into a direct cost centre. Disputes get longer and stranger: a creator can be charged on day 0, cancel on day 6, be refunded, and still file a chargeback; the platform is `losses_collector` either way. The platform becomes custodian of every open job's funds in its Stripe balance, which is an accounting and, in some places, a money-transmission optics question the founders' lawyer should hear about once. For the D-1 attacker nothing gets worse and one thing gets better: capture at post plus Checkout means Radar actually scores the payment, but the cash-out at award is exactly as fast as today unless a payout delay, a price ceiling and a dispute handler ship in the same change. Optional BNPL should not be in the first version: BNPL providers have their own refund and dispute rules, and an escrow product whose main creator action before award is "cancel and refund" is a poor fit for them.

**What must ship in the same change, not after.** The atomic status claim (D-3), because the same non-atomic state machine will now be holding real balances instead of holds; deterministic idempotency keys on every Stripe mutation and persisted `stripeChargeId`, `stripeTransferId`, `stripeRefundId` in the same write as each status change (D-3, D-7); webhook handlers for `checkout.session.completed` (the only trustworthy "paid" signal; the return URL is not), `checkout.session.expired`, `charge.refunded`, `charge.dispute.created` and `charge.dispute.closed`, each written as one `updateMany` with an allowed-from list (D-6); a real refund path with a `refunded` state and refund-failure handling (D-2) plus a written refund policy the Terms actually match; the price ceiling and a payout delay for first-time recipients (D-1); a `Payout` ledger for both Stripe and manual payouts (D-8); and a reconciliation view that lists every job whose Stripe state and DB state disagree, because with settled funds a disagreement is real money sitting somewhere. The job must be created in a `PENDING_PAYMENT` state before the redirect and activated only by the webhook, with `client_reference_id = jobId`, or abandoned sessions become ghost jobs. Move the sweeps to a cron in the same change (D-4); with settled funds there is no reason for money to move on a page load.

**Recommendation.** Do it, capture-on-post, and do it as the vehicle for the Week-1 state-machine work rather than as a feature bolted onto the current one. Prefer keeping the existing Payment Element with automatic capture (adding Link and Apple Pay there is configuration) unless the founders specifically want Checkout's hosted page; either way the webhook is the source of truth. Pair it with a product decision on cancellation (a short free-cancel window, then refund minus the Stripe fee, or the fee absorbed as a cost of doing business, but decided and written down) and with a decision on previews (D-11), because charging real money up front while giving away full-length MP3s and free cancellation is the worst combination of both. The alternative of "save the card at post, charge at award" (SetupIntent) avoids refunds and custody entirely but moves the failure to the moment the musician has already done the work, which is the wrong party to expose on a site built by musicians. Extended authorization (30 days on some cards) is not a fix, it is a longer timer.
